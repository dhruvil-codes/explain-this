Compound interest means your earnings start earning too. Each year, interest is added to your savings. Next year you earn interest on the larger sum.

Compare the two kinds:

- **Simple interest.** You earn the same amount every year. It applies only to your starting sum. $1,000 at 5% gains $50 each year.
- **Compound interest.** Earnings join your balance each year. Then the rate applies to a larger sum. That $1,000 reaches about $1,629 after ten years.

Three things shape the result:

- **Starting sum.** This is the money you begin with. It is called the principal.
- **Rate.** This is the yearly percent your savings earn.
- **Time.** This matters most. Each extra year multiplies all the growth before it.

Regular top-ups speed things up. Each deposit starts growing on its own.

For example, save $200 a month at 6% yearly for 30 years. You put in $72,000. The balance reaches roughly $201,000. About $129,000 of that is growth. Starting early beats saving more later, because early money has the most time to multiply.
